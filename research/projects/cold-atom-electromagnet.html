Electromagnet for Cold Atoms

Design, modelling, fabrication, and magnetic-field measurements for a bias-curvature electromagnet for ultracold lithium-6 experiments.

Project Abstract

This undergraduate research project and my senior-year thesis at Yale-NUS College explored a Bitter-type bias-curvature electromagnet for ultracold lithium-6 experiments. The bias coils provide the strong magnetic field that tunes atom interactions, while the smaller curvature coils shape the field profile and can compensate its curvature.

I used finite-element simulations to compare coil designs, then fabricated prototype coils and measured their magnetic-field profiles. These measurements supported a model of a strong, near-zero-curvature field, while full-power operation and the magnet’s cooling performance remained to be demonstrated.

Contribution & Recognition

This undergraduate research project and senior-year thesis combined electromagnet design, finite-element modelling, fabrication, and magnetic-field measurements. The project was supervised by Dr. Ben A. Olsen at Yale-NUS College.

Background And Motivation

Magnetic fields tune interactions in ultracold Fermi gases. The project was motivated by work studying transitions between dynamical phases in a quantum-degenerate Fermi gas.

Results And Next Steps

Finite-element simulations and prototype measurements supported a design for a strong, near-zero-curvature field. Full-power operation and cooling performance remained to be demonstrated.
