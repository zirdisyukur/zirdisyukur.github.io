Building and Testing a Radio Telescope

A portable 21-cm telescope project to characterise radio-frequency interference in Singapore and explore a teaching instrument for Yale-NUS College.

Project Abstract

Neutral atomic hydrogen emits strongly at a wavelength of 21 cm, making the line a useful tool for studying the structure and dynamics of nearby galaxies. This undergraduate summer research project improved a small, portable feed-horn radio telescope to characterise the radio-frequency interference environment in Singapore near 21 cm.

The work also considered whether a larger instrument on the Yale-NUS campus could support teaching. The project combined practical telescope construction with spectrum measurement and analysis.

Background And Motivation

Neutral hydrogen emits at a wavelength of 21 cm. Observing this line provides a way to study gas and galactic structure, while local radio-frequency interference can complicate measurements.
